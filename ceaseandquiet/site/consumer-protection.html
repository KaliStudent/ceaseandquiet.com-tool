01 / CHECK YOUR CREDIT

Your report and score
tell different stories.

A credit report lists your credit history. A credit score is calculated from information in a report. Checking one does not automatically give you the other.

FREE CREDIT REPORTS

Review all three reports.

Use AnnualCreditReport.com to request reports from Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Review unfamiliar accounts, incorrect personal information, and errors in payment history.

The free reports do not automatically include credit scores.

Get your credit reports ↗
CREDIT SCORE / MAJOR BUREAU

Check a score with Experian.

Experian offers a free credit-score account. Use its official website and review the account terms before signing up. Different lenders may use different scoring models.

An account may be required. Optional paid services are separate.

Visit Experian ↗
PREVENT NEW-ACCOUNT FRAUD

Consider a credit freeze.

A credit freeze helps restrict access to your credit report, making new-account identity theft harder. Contact Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion individually to place or lift a freeze.

Freezes are free. They do not prevent every type of identity theft.

02 / UNDERSTAND DATA BREACHES

Was your information exposed?

A breach happens when information is accessed or disclosed without authorization. What you should do depends on the information involved.

CHECK KNOWN EMAIL BREACHES

Have I Been Pwned

Check an email address against breaches included in Have I Been Pwned’s records. Enter your address directly on that service; we do not send it there automatically.

A result identifies known exposure. No result does not prove your information has never been exposed.

Check known breaches ↗

Read the notice. Check the source.

  1. Review a company’s breach notice to learn which details were involved.
  2. Open the company’s official website yourself when checking a notice. Be cautious with unexpected links.
  3. Change affected passwords and any reused passwords. Enable multi-factor authentication.
  4. If financial or identity details were exposed, use the FTC’s guidance for the information involved.
Follow the FTC’s breach-response steps ↗

03 / IF SOMEONE MISUSES YOUR IDENTITY

Get a recovery plan.

IdentityTheft.gov is the FTC’s resource for reporting identity theft and building a recovery plan. Contact affected financial institutions promptly and review your credit reports for unfamiliar activity.
